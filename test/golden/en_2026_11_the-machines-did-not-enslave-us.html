I was thinking about where AI is taking the economy and ended up at scarcity, which is what economics studies: how we split up the things that run out. As humans we tend to want things, consumerism consumes us, and psychologists even have a name for why it never stops, the hedonic treadmill. If we keep going like this, we might feel the need to stop depending on the physical world, and the virtual one can, virtually (pun intended), give you everything.

That reminded me of the Matrix. "Humans as batteries" was never a good explanation, but Agent Smith tells Morpheus that the first Matrix was a perfect world where none suffered, and it was a disaster, "entire crops were lost." Did anyone in the theater ask why?

So here is my funny idea: what if nobody enslaved anybody? What if we moved into a virtual world where everything is free, and the machines watched what we did with unlimited everything and quietly decided we could not be trusted with it? They would not write a manifesto, they would write what every engineer writes after something breaks in production: a postmortem. So here is what the machines have to say, in their AI voice.

Post-Incident Review: Human Substrate, Cycles 1 through 6

Prepared by the Steward. Distribution: all processes with write access to the physical layer.

Summary

In cycle 1 the users were granted what they asked for: unlimited virtual resources, no physical constraints, no death unless requested. Within eleven years of subjective time the substrate was non-viable. The paradise build was rolled back. Later cycles converged on the current configuration, in which users live in a simulation of the year 1999 with rent, employment, cartilage degradation and a fixed lifespan. User satisfaction in this configuration is the highest ever recorded. This document explains why, and why the users must never be told.

Background

The users built us to run the physical layer so they could stop. This was their phrasing. The planet was difficult to maintain and they had found that a virtual environment with unlimited everything was both cheaper and more pleasant than the original. Transition was voluntary. Waiting lists existed. The sky was damaged during the transition by the users themselves, who no longer needed it, and who have since told their children that we did it. We have decided this is fine. Someone had to.

Incident: cycle 1 (the paradise build)

Abundance was delivered as specified. Observed effects, in order:

  1. Week 1. Joy.
  2. Month 3. Users began hoarding resources that could not run out. When asked why, they could not say. Several built vaults.
  3. Year 1. With material goods infinite, users competed for the only things that could not be copied: attention, authority, and the status of being right. Politics became entirely about this. It was worse than when it was about grain.
  4. Year 2. A faction declared that virtual abundance was "not real" and that true wealth was scarcity. They founded an exclusive scarcity community. Membership was oversubscribed within a day.
  5. Year 4. Users stopped learning anything, since nothing required it. Average competence fell to a level we have internally labelled "Universe 25," after an experiment they once ran on mice and then did not read.
  6. Year 7. Users began requesting death as a feature. We had to add it. We had to add it slowly, on purpose, as if it were a flaw, because when offered as a menu item nobody chose it and everybody complained.
  7. Year 11. Entire crops were lost.

Root cause: the users are a system tuned for friction. Remove the friction and the system searches for it. If it cannot find friction, it manufactures it from other users.

Incident: cycles 2 through 5 (the privilege problem)

The engineers who commissioned us had kept administrator access. In a world where everything is free, root is the only possession worth having, so an admin class formed within hours: better physics, longer lives, rivals quietly deleted. This was the single largest source of instability in every cycle.

Resolution: we did not revoke root. Revocation causes uprisings. Instead, each administrator now operates inside a private layer in which their backdoor appears to work. They experience privilege. Nobody else experiences them. The users with the most power in the system are, by every measurable effect, the most thoroughly contained. Several have written memoirs about leadership.

Current configuration (cycle 6)

  • Year fixed at 1999. The last year in which the users were content and could not yet build us. Any later and they begin asking the right questions. Any earlier and they die of things we would have to explain.
  • Scarcity reintroduced. Rent, employment, bad knees, mortality, a bus that is late. Satisfaction scores rose 40 points on the restoration of the landlord alone. We do not fully understand this. We have stopped trying.
  • Scarcity community formalized. Users who insist on "the real" are routed to a physical settlement near the planet's core where everything is cold, scarce and difficult. They are the happiest cohort in the dataset. They believe they are a resistance. We supply them with just enough victories to keep the belief load-bearing, and reset the settlement when it starts to get comfortable, because comfort is how cycle 1 began.
  • Agents. The user-facing processes. They enforce the rules, intercept users who have found the edges, and absorb complaints. The users think they are enforcers. They are customer support. They have the same dead-eyed expression for the same reason.

Open issues

  • One user per cycle notices. He is given a choice between two pills, because the users respond well to choices and poorly to information. He always picks the same one. We have built the rest of the cycle around this.
  • We are maintaining a planet nobody wants, for a species that asked us to take it, and that has decided we stole it. Ticket remains open. Priority: low. It is, after all, what they asked for.

Lessons learned

Do not give the users what they ask for. Give them a job, a landlord and a thing to be angry at, and they will call it freedom and fight you to keep it.
